Summary of O-RAN’s AI/ML Workflow Perspective

Architecture, Interfaces, Deployment Scenarios & Research Gaps

Published

June 23, 2026

References: Polese, Bonati, D’Oro, Basagni, Melodia, “Understanding O-RAN: Architecture, Interfaces, Algorithms, Security, and Research Challenges” (arXiv:2202.01032, 2022). This document [reports the content of the paper with AI/ML workflow as the axis rearrange and the research gap derived therefrom Organize together.

1 Introduction: Why the “AI/ML Workflow” Axis?

The essence of O-RAN lies in disaggregating base stations and opening them up to standard interfaces, but the purpose of its existence is to enable the deployment of ML to RAN control. Therefore, if O-RAN is viewed not as “disaggregating boxes” but as a matter of “how to place an ML pipeline on which node of the network”, the architecture, interfaces, and deployment scenarios align into a single consistent picture.

From this perspective, the core questions can be condensed into two.

  1. What functional stages make up an AI/ML workflow?
  2. Each step which node (SMO / Non-RT RIC / Near-RT RIC / RAN node) can be placed at |||, and what is the constraint that determines the choice?

The decisive variable determining the answer to the second question is timescale , and this is the axis that runs through the entire document.

2 Background: O-RAN control layer hosting AI/ML

we organize the control layer that defines “where” the workflow is placed based on the time scale.

If this hierarchical structure is formalized as a nested control loop, the outer loop is slower and global, while the inner loop is faster and local. When the control cycle is denoted as \(T_\ell\), approximately

\[ T_{\text{Non-RT}} \;>\; 1\,\text{s} \;\gg\; T_{\text{Near-RT}} \in [10\,\text{ms},\,1\,\text{s}] \;\gg\; T_{\text{RT}} \;<\; 10\,\text{ms} \]

holds. <10 ms real-time loops are an area not standardized in the current specification (further study), and this fact is directly related to the subsequent research gap.

3 Lifecycle Stages of AI/ML Workflow

The AI/ML lifecycle defined by O-RAN WG2 is a cyclic pipeline from data to inference, consisting of the following stages.

  1. Data Preparation — Collection · Preprocessing · Feature Engineering
  2. AI/ML Training — Model Training (Offline is generally required)
  3. Validation & Deployment — Deploy to target nodes after validation
  4. Inference — Perform real-time inference with a trained model
  5. Continuous Operations — Post-deployment performance monitoring and retraining triggers

The core operating principles are as follows.

  • Offline training required: Discontinue the discontinuation of unverified models. Online learning is permitted only for fine-tuning purposes.
  • Current Limit of Inference Host: Inference is limited to RIC in the current specification, and Inference in CU/DU is in a pending (further study) state.

4.2 Scenario Interpretation

  • 1.1 — Fully Centralized: All functions concentrated in a Non-RT RIC. Inference >Since it is on a 1 s scale, it is suitable for slow decisions such as slicing policies and traffic forecasting. Fast closed-loop control impossible.

  • 1.2 — Separation of Training/Inference: Training is Non-RT (Offline), Inference is Near-RT descends to form a 10 ms–1 s closed loop. The most commonly discussed structure.

  • 1.3 — Separation of Authority: Model Management and Training are owned by SMO, Operation and inference are handled by Non-RT. A structure in which a central SMO deploys a common model in a large-scale network and local Non-RTs perform inference.

  • 1.4 — Online Adaptive Loop: Unlike 1.2, up to Continuous Operations Near-RT** is in charge. The model self-monitors in real-time and requests retraining from Non-RT if performance degrades. It has the highest responsiveness among RIC-based scenarios.

  • 1.5 (FFS) — RAN Node Inference: Inference is executed on the O-CU/O-DU itself. The only structure that enables real-time control of the TTI scale, but is not yet standardized (For Further Study).

How to Read: In Scenario 1.5, which of the SMO·Near-RT RICs is empty in the table It is not “not needed”, but rather since it is FFS, the placement of that function is simply undefined. Two controllers are always present in the network for FCAPS management, RRM, etc.

6 Use Cases Where ML Is Actually Applied

Among the ML application areas classified by the paper, the key points from the perspective of resource control are as follows.

  • Resource Allocation (Network Slicing/Scheduling) — ML Application Most active area. Numerous closed-loop slicing control examples (ColO-RAN, NexRAN, etc.).
  • Mobility — Handover/Load Balancing Prediction.
  • QoE/QoS Control — Resource adjustment based on user perceived quality.
  • RAN Sharing / Massive MIMO / New Applications(UAV·Vehicle·Industrial IoT).

Among these, scheduling is the region with the fastest time scale, so it is highlighted that only 1.5 (in-node inference) is timing-appropriate in the scenario table above.

7 Research Gap

We address the gaps explicitly and implicitly left by the paper from the perspective of AI/ML workflow. Everything converges around Scenario 1.5 (In-node Inference).

7.1 Gap 1 — Real-time (<10 ms) Absence of inference loop

Since the inference host is limited to RIC (≥10 ms), there is no standard path to directly apply ML to fast control at the TTI scale (scheduling, link adaptation). Formally, for in-node inference to be possible, the sum of the inference delay \(\tau_{\text{inf}}\) and the scheduling processing delay \(\tau_{\text{sched}}\) must not exceed the TTI budget .

\[ \tau_{\text{inf}} + \tau_{\text{sched}} \;\le\; \text{TTI} \quad (\text{TTI} \approx 0.5\text{–}1\,\text{ms}) \]

It is not verified whether this inequality holds in the current hardware and model structure, and this is the first technical reason why 1.5 is FFS.

7.2 Gap 2 — Absence of model delivery interface

the 1.5 architecture where “Model Management is Non-RT and Inference is O-DU,” a standard interface to directly drop trained model weights from Non-RT to the node is required. However, the current interface serves a different purpose.

7.3 Gap 3 — Resource isolation

In-node inference implies that RAN functions and ML workloads share the same hardware . Consequently, (i) contention for GPU/CPU resources, (ii) jitter that undermines RAN real-time performance, and (iii) the absence of QoS-guaranteed resource allocation policies remain issues . A structure for dynamic separation and allocation of RAN workloads and AI inference workloads has not been defined.

7.4 Gap 4 — Lack of utilization of cross-layer signals

Cross-layer mapping that propagates signals from model layers (e.g., token importance of generative models — entropy, logprob, attention) as utility inputs to MAC schedulers is an area rarely covered in either standards or literature. This gains significance when combined with Gap 1 (fast control + model-derived signals).

8 Summary: Alignment to research positioning

The gaps above converge on a single axis called the unrealized scenario 1.5, and research demonstrating this naturally organizes into hierarchical control .

  • The Slow Layer (RIC) supplies policies, weights, and predictions to the Fast Layer.
  • Fast layer (node) directly executes inference + scheduling at the TTI scale.

In this structure, the systematic contribution of the study lies in the third row — the inference + scheduling loop that actually runs at the node — which corresponds to the first demonstration of 1.5 remaining as FFS. The necessary technical elements (channel quality prediction, traffic prediction) are each mapped as inputs to the Near-RT layer, and the verification environment (GH200 / pyAerial / cuMAC / ROS2) is mapped as the actual answer for Gap 1·3.

9 One-line summary

The O-RAN AI/ML workflow is a problem of deploying 5 functions to 4 nodes, and The variable that determines that choice is the time scale. Descending inference to the RAN node Only Scenario 1.5 enables TTI scale control, but it still remains as FFS. exists, and precisely this unrealized point is cross-layer·systems-level research It is the core void to be placed.
